The sun hangs low on the horizon, a bruised‑purple globe bleeding thin bands of amber and rose into the sky. Its light stretches thin across the sea, catching the crests of the waves in fleeting, molten gold before they dissolve back into the darkening water.

Jagged cliffs rise from the shore like ancient stone ribs, their faces pitted and weather‑worn by relentless surf and salty wind. Layers of sediment—strata of ochre, slate, and rust‑red—are striped across the cliffs, each band telling a story of ages long past. In places the rock has been gnawed away into sheer overhangs and narrow ledges, where seabirds perch in tight, restless clusters, their silhouettes outlined against the waning light.

The tide pools cling to the foot of the cliffs like scattered mirrors, each one a miniature world of glimmering algae, star‑shaped sea stars, and darting crabs. As the dusk deepens, the pools reflect the sky’s soft bruised hues, their surfaces trembling with each gentle lap of the surf.

A thin ribbon of foam rolls endlessly over the pebbled beach, the sand a patchwork of dark basalt fragments, smooth sea‑glass, and remnants of shell. The tide pulls back in rhythmic sighs, exposing a wet, reflective carpet that catches the last flickers of daylight, turning the shoreline into a glassy hallway that seems to lead directly into the horizon.

Farther out, the ocean itself becomes a vast, indigo canvas, broken only by the occasional crest that catches a glimmer of the dying sun. A faint, salty mist rises from the surf, curling around the cliff edges and drifting inland, smearing the air with the scent of brine and wet stone.

Above the cliffs, the first shy stars prick the deepening sky, their cold light mingling with the lingering glow of sunset. A lone gull circles above, its call a brief, echoing note that cuts through the hush of the evening. The world feels suspended between day’s warm farewell and the quiet promise of night, the rocky coast standing resolute, timeless, and beautifully stark against the fading light.
