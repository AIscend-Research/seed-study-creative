A teapot is a vessel designed specifically for steeping tea leaves (or tea bags) in hot water and then serving the brewed tea. While its exact appearance can vary widely across cultures and styles, a classic teapot typically has the following components and characteristics:

---

### Overall Shape
- **Body (or kettle):** A rounded, bulbous chamber that holds the water and tea leaves. It’s usually wider at the base and tapers slightly toward the top, creating a smooth, graceful silhouette.
- **Spout:** A narrow, curved tube extending from the side near the top of the body, ending in a tapered tip. The spout directs the flow of liquid, allowing you to pour the tea without dripping.
- **Lid:** A fitted cover that sits snugly on the top of the body. It often has a small knob or handle for easy removal, and sometimes a short handle on the lid itself for lifting.
- **Handle:** A sturdy, often curved or looped handle attached to the side of the body opposite the spout. It provides a secure grip for pouring when the teapot is hot.

---

### Common Materials
| Material | Appearance & Feel | Typical Use |
|----------|-------------------|-------------|
| **Ceramic/Porcelain** | Smooth, glossy finish; can be painted or glazed with intricate designs. | Everyday home use; often decorative. |
| **Cast Iron (Yixing style)** | Dark, matte surface; heavy and resilient; may develop a patina over time. | Traditional Chinese tea ceremony; excellent heat retention. |
| **Stainless Steel** | Brushed or polished metal; sleek, modern look. | Commercial settings or minimalist kitchens. |
| **Glass** | Transparent or frosted; allows you to watch the tea infusing. | Visual appeal; often used for herbal or fruit teas. |
| **Clay (Earthenware)** | Earthy, porous texture; can absorb flavors over time. | Specialty teas that benefit from subtle flavor enhancement. |

---

### Functional Details
- **Infuser/Strainer:** Many modern teapots include a built-in stainless‑steel or fine‑mesh infuser that sits inside the body to hold loose tea leaves while allowing water to circulate. Some traditional designs rely on a separate, removable strainer.
- **Heat Retention:** The thickness of the material and the presence of a lid help keep the tea warm after steeping. Cast iron and thick ceramic teapots excel at this.
- **Aesthetic Features:** Teapots can be adorned with motifs (flowers, animals, calligraphy), embossed patterns, or color gradients. In many cultures, the teapot itself is a piece of art reflecting local craftsmanship.

---

### Typical Sizes
- **Personal/Singles:** 8–12 oz (≈ 250–350 ml) – ideal for one or two servings.
- **Family/Small Group:** 16–24 oz (≈ 500–750 ml) – common for everyday household use.
- **Entertaining:** 32 oz and larger (≈ 1 L+) – used for gatherings or formal tea services.

---

### Example Description (Classic English Porcelain Teapot)

Imagine a porcelain teapot glazed in a soft ivory with delicate blue daisy petals scattered across its surface. The body is a gentle, rounded sphere, slightly wider at the base, giving it a stable, balanced feel on the table. A smooth, slightly curved spout arches gracefully from the right side, its tip perfectly tapered for a clean pour. Opposite the spout, a gracefully looping handle arches outward, its interior polished to a subtle shine that catches the light. The round lid, crowned with a tiny silver knob, fits snugly, sealing in heat while allowing steam to escape in a delicate wispy curl. When lifted, the teapot feels warm but manageable, and as you tilt it, the tea flows in a thin, steady stream, unfurling a fragrant aroma of bergamot and dried leaves.

---

### Cultural Touchstones
- **Japanese “Kyusu”:** A side‑handle teapot often made of clay, with a built-in screen and a short spout for precise pouring.
- **Chinese “Yixing” teapot:** Made from porous Yixing clay, prized for its ability to absorb tea flavors and improve over years of use.
- **British “Brown Betty”:** A quintessential teapot with a simple, rounded shape and a ceramic “baked” finish, traditionally used for English Breakfast tea.

---

In essence, a teapot is both a functional kitchen tool and, often, a decorative object that reflects the aesthetics, traditions, and tea‑drinking customs of the culture it comes from. Whether you’re brewing a robust Assam, a delicate green tea, or an herbal infusion, the teapot is the vessel that brings the water, leaf, and heat together to create the comforting ritual of tea.
