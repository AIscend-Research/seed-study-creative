The sun is slipping behind the horizon, and the sky is washed in a bruised‑purple glow that deepens in the west and fades to soft, amber‑pink near the water’s edge. A thin veil of mist rolls in from the sea, catching the last golden shafts of light and turning them into faint, trembling ribbons that drift lazily over the surf.

Jagged cliffs rise like weather‑worn sentinels along the shoreline, their dark basalt faces pocked with gullies and streaked with salt‑stained lichen. In the fading light, the rock takes on layers of color: deep charcoal at the base, banded with rust‑red iron stains, and flecks of pale, sun‑bleached stone that glimmer like shattered glass. Between the outcrops, narrow ledges tumble down to the beach, slick with sea spray, and the occasional wave crashes against them, sending up frothy white arches that quickly dissolve into the sea’s calm sigh.

The tide pools nestle in the hollows of the cliffs, reflecting the twilight sky like miniature mirrors. Tiny ripples disturb their surfaces as curious crabs scuttle back and forth, their shells catching the dimming light. Seabirds, silhouettes against the violet sky, wheel in lazy circles before slipping back to roost on the rocky ribs, their calls echoing faintly over the steady hush of the surf.

Further out, the ocean spreads like a dark, glassy plain, its surface mottled with the last glints of sun‑fire. Each gentle swell rolls toward the shore in a slow, rhythmic procession, breaking softly against the base of the cliffs and leaving a faint, salty perfume in the cool evening air. The horizon line blurs where sea meets sky, a seamless band of deep indigo that promises the night to come.

As dusk deepens, the first stars prick the heavens, and a pale, milky crescent moon climbs, casting a silvered sheen across the water and tracing a faint, luminous path along the cliffs' edges. The landscape, etched in stone and sea, seems both ancient and fleeting, a quiet testimony to the endless dance of light, water, and wind at the edge of day.
