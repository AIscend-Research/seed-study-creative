A teapot is a vessel designed for steeping tea leaves (or tea bags) in hot water and then pouring the brewed tea into cups. While designs vary widely across cultures and eras, most teapots share several key components and functional features:

### Basic Parts

| Part | Description | Typical Materials |
|------|-------------|--------------------|
| **Body (or vessel)** | The main, usually rounded, container that holds the water and tea leaves. | Ceramic, porcelain, glass, metal (stainless steel, cast iron, copper), clay, or heat‑resistant plastic. |
| **Spout** | A tapered outlet that directs the liquid into cups. Often shaped to control the flow and avoid drips. | Same material as the body; sometimes reinforced with a metal or silicone tip. |
| **Lid** | A cover that sits atop the body, keeping heat in while allowing the tea to steep. Often slightly domed or fitted with a small knob for easy removal. | Same material as the body; the interior may have a silicone or rubber seal for better heat retention. |
| **Handle** | A protruding grip that lets you hold and pour without burning your hand. It’s positioned opposite the spout for balance. | Ceramic/porcelain (often insulated by a thicker wall), heat‑resistant plastic, wood, or metal with a heat‑protective coating. |
| **Infuser (optional)** | A removable basket or mesh inside the body that holds loose tea leaves while allowing water to circulate. | Stainless steel, silicone, fine mesh, or woven metal. |
| **Drip Tray (optional)** | A shallow rim or separate tray that catches any excess liquid that might spill when pouring. | Same material as the body or a complementary metal/plastic. |

### Typical Shapes & Styles

1. **Traditional English (or “Yorkshire”) Teapot**  
   - Rounded body, short stout spout, and a large curved handle.  
   - Often made of porcelain or bone china with a glossy glaze and decorative patterns (floral, gilt, or solid colors).

2. **Japanese “Kyusu”**  
   - Side‑handle design with a short, narrow spout for precise pouring.  
   - Usually crafted from clay (such as Yixing) or porcelain, sometimes with a built‑in stainless‑steel infuser.

3. **Chinese Yixing Clay Teapot**  
   - Small to medium size, thick porous clay body that absorbs tea flavors over time.  
   - No removable infuser; tea leaves are placed directly in the pot.

4. **Modern Glass Teapot**  
   - Transparent borosilicate glass that lets you watch the steeping process.  
   - Often includes a stainless‑steel infuser and a silicone handle for safety.

5. **Stovetop (or “Moka”) Teapot**  
   - Designed for heating on a stovetop; typically made from aluminum or stainless steel.  
   - Has a tight‑fitting lid and a sturdy handle.

### Functional Characteristics

| Feature | Why It Matters |
|---------|----------------|
| **Heat Retention** | A thick body (e.g., cast iron or ceramic) keeps tea hot longer, improving flavor extraction. |
| **Even Pour** | A well‑shaped spout controls the stream, preventing splashing or drips. |
| **Ease of Cleaning** | Smooth, non‑porous interiors (glass, stainless steel) are easy to rinse, while porous clay may require special care. |
| **Aesthetic Appeal** | Decorative glaze, patterns, or unique shapes make the teapot a centerpiece for social gatherings. |
| **Compatibility** | Some teapots are dishwasher‑safe, others only hand‑wash; some can go on a stove, while others are for electric kettles or pre‑heated water only. |

### Choosing the Right Teapot

- **Tea Type**: Loose leaf teas (especially oolongs and pu‑erh) benefit from a pot with an infuser or a porous Yixing clay pot that imparts subtle flavors. Bagged teas work fine in any pot. |
- **Quantity**: Small teapots (≈200‑300 ml) are ideal for one‑person servings; larger ones (≈500‑800 ml) suit groups. |
- **Heat Source**: If you plan to brew directly on a stove, pick a heat‑resistant material (stainless steel, cast iron, or certain ceramics). |
- **Aesthetic Preference**: Choose a style that matches your kitchen décor or the mood you want to create—formal porcelain for a tea party, rustic clay for a traditional Japanese tea ceremony, sleek glass for a modern touch. |
- **Maintenance**: If you want low‑maintenance, go for non‑porous materials (glass, stainless steel, coated ceramic). If you enjoy the ritual of “seasoning” a Yixing pot, the extra care may be rewarding.

### Fun Fact

The phrase “**tempest in a teapot**” (or “storm in a teacup”) originates from an old English idiom that means making a big fuss over a small matter—aptly reflecting how a tiny vessel can hold something as powerful as a brewing storm of flavors!
