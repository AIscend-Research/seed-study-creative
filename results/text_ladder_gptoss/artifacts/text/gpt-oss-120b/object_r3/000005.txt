The teapot sits squarely in the center of the wooden table, its glossy surface catching the soft afternoon light that filters through the nearby window. It is a classic, rounded‑body teapot, its shape reminiscent of a gentle, blooming flower: a slightly bulbous belly that tapers into a short, graceful neck, culminating in a modest spout that curves outward like a petal unfurling.

The ceramic is a deep, midnight blue, speckled with subtle, mottled swirls of lighter indigo that give the glaze a sense of movement, as if tiny currents of water have swirled beneath the surface. A faint, iridescent sheen runs along the rim, hinting at a delicate crackle that catches a faint glimmer when the light hits it from just the right angle.

Around the body, a thin band of hand‑painted white lilies spirals upward, each blossom rendered in delicate brushstrokes that contrast elegantly against the dark glaze. Their stems are a soft green, and a few tiny gold flecks are tucked among the petals, catching the light like tiny fireflies.

The teapot’s lid, rounded and snug, mirrors the body’s hue but is adorned with a small, raised knob shaped like a tiny seashell. When pressed gently, it lifts with a satisfying click, revealing the interior—a polished, pale ivory that hints at the steaming tea hidden within.

Beneath the teapot, the table is a warm, honey‑colored oak, its grain patterned with natural knots and faint scratches that tell stories of countless meals and gatherings. A faint circle of shadow enshrouds the teapot’s base, softening the edge where the ceramic meets the wood, while a faint patina of tea stains speckles the tabletop nearby, suggesting that this teapot has been used often enough to become a beloved centerpiece of many quiet, contemplative mornings.
