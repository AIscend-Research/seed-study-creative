The sun is pulling its last, amber‑gold threads through a sky smeared with bruised purples and deepening blues. Along the edge of the sea, jagged cliffs rise like ancient sentinels, their dark basalt faces pocked with weather‑worn fissures and lichen‑spotted patches that catch the faint glow of the dying light. Waves tumble against the rocks in steady, rhythmic thuds, sending spray that catches the dusk air like a fleeting veil of silver.

At the base of the cliffs, a narrow, pebble‑strewn beach stretches outward, its stones ranging from smooth, sea‑polished glassy grays to rough, angular shards of quartz and volcanic rock. The tide’s receding line leaves a thin, glossy ribbon of water that mirrors the sky’s color‑shift, turning the sea into a giant, trembling sheet of indigo.

Scattered among the rocks, hardy coastal plants cling stubbornly to life: wind‑bent gnarled shrubs, tufts of sea‑grass swaying in the salty breeze, and occasional splashes of bright orange or red‑tipped wildflowers that seem to defiantly paint the stark landscape. Their silhouettes are soft charcoal against the glowing horizon.

Above, gulls wheel and call, their white bodies fading against the deepening twilight. The distant horizon is a thin, luminous line where the sea meets the sky, now a muted violet that promises the coming night. As the last sliver of sun sinks, the cliffs absorb the remaining warmth, turning their edges a soft, rosy pink before they too surrender to the cool, dusky blue of evening. The whole scene feels simultaneously timeless and fleeting—a perfect balance of rugged stone, restless water, and the quiet hush that settles in as day turns to night.
