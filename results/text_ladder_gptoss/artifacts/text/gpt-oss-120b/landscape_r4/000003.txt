At dusk the cliff‑rimmed coast glows in a muted amber, the sky smeared with bruised purples and pinks that bleed into the restless sea. Jagged outcrops of weather‑worn rock jut out like ancient sentinels, their edges softened by the waning light and the spray of frothy waves that crash against them. Perched atop the headland, a stout lighthouse stands watch, its white tower a stark silhouette against the twilight, its lantern casting a soft, steady beam that flickers like a lone eye scanning the horizon for the night’s first ships. The air is cool and salty, and the distant cry of gulls fades into the hush that settles over the shoreline as the day surrenders to night.
