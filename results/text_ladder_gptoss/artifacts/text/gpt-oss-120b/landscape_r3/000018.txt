The sun is slipping behind the horizon, pulling a thin line of molten gold across the restless sea. The sky, a bruised palette of deep indigo and fading violet, is streaked with faint pinks that linger for only a moment before giving way to the first cool shadows of night.

Jagged cliffs rise from the water like weather‑worn stone sentinels, their faces scarred with deep fissures and pockets where sea spray has turned the rock a glossy, slate‑gray. Dark, lichen‑green moss clings to the sheltered nooks, while hardy shrubs and scrubby heathers cling stubbornly to the thin soils that cling to the ledges. Between the boulders, narrow crevices snap open with the tide, revealing the shimmering black mirrors of tide pools that reflect the dwindling light.

The surf rolls in relentless, a grey‑white ribbon that crashes against the base of the cliffs, sending up salty mist that catches the waning sunlight in fleeting, pearlescent arcs. Occasionally a lone gull wheels above, its silhouette a stark, solitary brushstroke against the dimming firmament.

Further out, the sea flattens into a dark glass, broken only by the occasional rolling swell that catches a glimmer of orange before being swallowed again. The horizon is a thin, hazy line where the water meets the sky, both now deepening into midnight blue.

A cool breeze drifts off the water, carrying with it the scent of brine and wet stone. The soundscape is a symphony of low, rhythmic waves, the distant call of a gull, and the faint, metallic echo of rocks shifting under the pressure of the tide. As night settles, the first stars begin to prick the heavens, their faint light reflected in the darker pools at the cliff’s foot, turning the whole scene into a quiet, timeless tableau of rock, water, and the waning day.
