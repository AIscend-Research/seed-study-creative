A teapot is a small, usually porcelain, ceramic, metal, glass, or sometimes even wooden vessel designed specifically for steeping tea leaves in hot water and then serving the brewed tea. Its form is both functional and often decorative, and it typically includes several key components:

| Part | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Body (or Vessel)** | The main, rounded or slightly tapered container that holds the water and tea leaves. It may have a sleek, streamlined silhouette or a more ornate, curvilinear shape, depending on the style. |
| **Spout** | A tapered, often slightly curved outlet that allows the tea to be poured smoothly. The spout’s interior is usually smooth to prevent drips, and many teapots feature a “lip” or “siphon” that helps break surface tension, reducing splatter. |
| **Handle** | A sturdy, ergonomically placed grip (often on the opposite side of the spout) that lets you lift and tilt the teapot safely while it’s hot. Handles can be simple loops, intricate scrollwork, or even sculptural elements. |
| **Lid** | A removable cover that fits snugly on top of the body. It may have a knob or a small decorative finial, and sometimes includes a small opening for steam to escape, which helps control the brewing temperature. |
| **Infuser (optional)** | Some modern teapots contain a built‑in stainless‑steel mesh or silicone basket that holds loose tea leaves, keeping them separate from the liquid while allowing water to circulate freely. |
| **Base** | The flat bottom that keeps the teapot stable on a surface. In many traditional designs, the base is slightly wider than the body to improve balance. |

### Visual Characteristics
- **Materials:** Classic teapots are often made from porcelain or stoneware, which retain heat well and can be glazed in an endless array of colors and patterns. Metal teapots—such as those of cast iron (known as “tetsubin” in Japan) or silver—offer a distinct heft and heat conductivity. Glass teapots let you watch the tea steep, while wooden or bamboo models bring an earthy aesthetic.
- **Shape:** The overall silhouette can range from sleek, minimalist forms (think of a simple white porcelain teapot with clean lines) to ornate, curvaceous designs (such as a Chinese Yixing teapot with a dragon motif or a Victorian silver teapot with elaborate scrollwork).
- **Decoration:** Surface embellishments may include hand‑painted scenes, embossing, engraved patterns, gilt accents, or even artistic motifs that reflect cultural heritage (e.g., cherry blossoms for Japanese styles, lotus flowers for Chinese teas, or floral motifs for English “afternoon” teapots).

### Functional Aspects
1. **Steeping:** When hot water is poured over loose tea leaves or a tea bag inside the body, the leaves release flavors and tannins over a few minutes. The lid helps retain heat, while the shape of the body promotes even circulation of water around the leaves.
2. **Serving:** Once the tea is brewed to the desired strength, the teapot is tilted using its handle, and the tea flows through the spout into cups. A well‑designed spout minimizes drips and provides a steady, controlled pour.
3. **Heat Retention:** Materials like thick ceramic, cast iron, or double‑walled glass help keep the tea warm longer, allowing guests to enjoy a leisurely cup.

### Cultural Touchpoints
- **Chinese Yixing Teapots:** Made from a special clay that absorbs tea oils, improving flavor over time. They are often unglazed and handcrafted.
- **Japanese Tetsubin:** Cast‑iron teapots that are both functional and decorative, sometimes featuring intricate lacquer work on the exterior.
- **English Afternoon Teapot:** Usually porcelain with a floral pattern, reflecting the tradition of serving tea with milk and sugar in elegant porcelain cups.

In summary, a teapot is a thoughtfully engineered vessel that blends practicality—steeping and pouring tea efficiently—with artistic expression, often embodying the cultural aesthetics of the region where it was crafted. Its components work together to create an inviting ritual of preparing and sharing tea.
