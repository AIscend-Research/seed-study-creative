The sun has just begun to slip behind the horizon, turning the sky into a sprawling canvas of molten gold, fiery orange, and bruised violet. Ahead, a gentle river winds like a silver ribbon through the valley, its surface catching the dying light and scattering it in a thousand trembling shards. On either side of the water, low, rolling hills rise and fall in smooth, verdant waves, their grasses whispering in the soft evening breeze. 

At the far end of the valley, a jagged line of mountains looms—ancient, snow‑capped peaks that catch the last pink blush of daylight on their lofty ridges. Their shadows stretch down the slopes, mingling with the dark silhouettes of pines that stand like sentinels, their needles rustling with each sigh of wind. 

Near the riverbank, a meadow spreads out, speckled with wildflowers that have just closed their petals for the night. Daisies, lavender, and clover create a muted tapestry of purples and whites, while a lone oak tree spreads its broad, gnarled limbs over a patch of soft earth, its bark rough and honey‑brown. A family of deer steps cautiously out of the tree line, their ears flicking as they drink from the cool water, eyes reflecting the amber glow of the sky.

Above, the first stars prick the deepening blue, tiny beacons that will soon outshine the fading sunset. A lone hawk circles lazily on the thermals, its silhouette a dark brushstroke against the painted heavens. The air is crisp, tinged with the scent of pine resin and the faint, earthy perfume of damp soil, promising the cool hush of night that will soon settle over this tranquil, ever‑watching landscape.
