The sun is slipping behind the horizon, casting the sky in a wash of bruised‑purple and amber‑gold. Thin, wispy clouds catch the last glints of light, turning their edges flaming pink before they dissolve into the deepening twilight.

Below, the coastline is a jagged fringe of weather‑worn rock. Massive slabs of basalt and granite rise abruptly from the sea, their surfaces rough and pitted, streaked with salt‑stained moss and lichen that cling like dark‑green tattoos. Some of the larger outcrops are split by fissures that reveal darker, glossy interiors, while smaller tide‑pools glitter with reflected light, their surfaces rippling with the gentle lap of the incoming tide.

The waves crash against the cliffs in a steady, thudding rhythm, sending up sprays of sea‑foam that catch the dying light and momentarily sparkle like scattered diamonds. The roar of the surf mixes with the distant call of gulls circling high above, their silhouettes stark against the fading sky.

Along the lower ledges, hardy grasses and low‑lying shrubs sway in a cool breeze that carries the briny tang of the ocean. Pockets of seaweed cling to the rocks, their fronds swaying like dark, rustling ribbons. In the deeper crevices, the water glows a muted turquoise, hinting at the life that thrives beneath the surface.

As dusk deepens, the sky shifts to deeper blues, and the first stars begin to prick the heavens. A thin veil of mist rolls in from the sea, curling around the base of the cliffs and softening the harsh outlines of the rock. The world feels both vast and intimate—a place where stone, sea, and sky meet in a quiet, awe‑inspiring transition from day to night.
