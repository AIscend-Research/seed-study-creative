A teapot is a charming, functional piece of kitchenware designed to brew and serve hot beverages—most famously tea. Its core components include:

**1. Body (the kettle)**  
- **Shape:** Often rounded or slightly bulbous, the body holds water and the steeping tea leaves. Its smooth, curving sides help distribute heat evenly.  
- **Material:** Classic teapots are made of ceramic (porcelain, stoneware, or earthenware), but you’ll also find glass, stainless steel, cast iron, and even enamel‑coated aluminum models. Each material influences heat retention and flavor purity.  

**2. Lid**  
- **Fit:** A snug, dome‑shaped lid keeps steam inside, maintaining temperature while the brew steeps. Many lids have a small knob or handle for easy lifting.  
- **Vent:** Some lids feature a tiny opening or a removable "tea infuser" that allows excess steam to escape, preventing pressure buildup.

**3. Spout**  
- **Design:** The spout is tapered to pour a steady, controlled stream without dribbling. Its angle and length vary—some teapots have a long, elegant pour, while others favor a short, functional spout.  
- **Lip:** A thin, smooth lip reduces splatter, making it easy to serve tea into cups or mugs.

**4. Handle**  
- **Ergonomics:** Positioned opposite the spout, the handle (often an arched “C” or a loop) allows you to lift and tilt the teapot comfortably, even when the contents are hot.  
- **Insulation:** In metal teapots, the handle is sometimes made of a heat‑resistant material (wood, plastic, or a thick metal gut) to keep your hand safe.

**5. Interior (optional accessories)**  
- **Infuser or Basket:** Many modern teapots include a removable stainless‑steel or silicone mesh basket that holds loose leaf tea while allowing water to circulate.  
- **Strainer:** Some designs have a built-in perforated strainer at the spout, so you can pour directly without a separate strainer.

**Aesthetic touches**  
- **Decoration:** Traditional designs may feature hand‑painted motifs, floral patterns, or intricate blue‑and‑white porcelain scenes. Contemporary teapots might sport minimalist lines, matte finishes, or bold, monochrome colors.  
- **Size:** Ranges from tiny “one‑cup” teapots (about 250 ml) for personal use to large, family‑size models holding 1–2 liters.

**Functionality in use**  
1. **Heat the water:** Fill the body with cold water and bring it to a boil (or to the appropriate temperature for the tea type).  
2. **Steep:** Add tea leaves (or place the infuser), pour the hot water over them, and let the brew steep for the recommended time.  
3. **Serve:** Remove the infuser (or let the tea settle), then pour the liquid through the spout into cups, enjoying the aroma and flavor.

In essence, a teapot blends practicality with artistry—a vessel that not only prepares a comforting drink but also often serves as a decorative focal point on a kitchen countertop or dining table.
