The sky is a bruised violet, the last orange‑pink streaks of the sun slipping behind a thin line of distant hills. A soft, salty breeze drifts over the cliffs, carrying the faint, metallic tang of sea‑foam and the distant cry of gulls returning to roost.

The coastline is jagged, a series of sheer basalt heads that have been carved by relentless waves into sharp, angular forms. Dark, weather‑worn rocks rise like the broken ribs of an ancient leviathan, their surfaces pocked with sea‑caves and hidden fissures that swallow echoing winds. In the lower tiers, tide‑pools cling to the rock face, their glassy surfaces reflecting the dimming light like miniature mirrors, teeming with tiny, scuttling crabs and the occasional flicker of a star‑fish.

Where the cliffs meet the water, waves crash in relentless, foamy crescents, sending up sprays that catch the fading glow and briefly turn silver. The surf pulls back in rhythmic sighs, leaving a damp sheen of sea‑salt on the stone, which glistens like wet slate under the emerging twilight.

Clusters of scrubby, wind‑bent shrubs and gnarled, knobby bushes cling to the thinner patches of soil, their silvery leaves trembling in the evening air. A lone, twisted pine leans against the cliff’s edge, its dark silhouette a steadfast sentinel against the encroaching night.

Above, the first stars prick the sky, their faint light reflected in the rippling surface of the water, while a thin, crescent moon rises, casting a pale, silvery band across the horizon. The whole scene is wrapped in a hushed, cool stillness, the day's heat giving way to the crisp, salty air of night, and the rugged coastline stands as a timeless, stoic witness to the day's final sigh.
