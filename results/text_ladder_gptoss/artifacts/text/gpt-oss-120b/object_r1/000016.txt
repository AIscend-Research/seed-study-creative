A teapot is a vessel designed specifically for steeping tea leaves (or tea bags) in hot water and then pouring the brewed tea into cups. Though its shape can vary widely across cultures and design styles, a classic teapot typically includes the following elements:

| Part | Description |
|------|--------------|
| **Body (or vessel)** | The main, usually rounded or slightly bulbous, container that holds the water and tea leaves. It’s often made of ceramic, porcelain, glass, metal (like stainless steel or cast iron), or even clay. The body may be decorated with patterns, glazes, or engravings. |
| **Lid** | A snug-fitting cover that rests on the top of the body. It keeps heat inside while the tea steeps. Many lids have a small knob or handle for easy removal, and some feature a tiny hole to release steam. |
| **Spout** | A tapered outlet on one side of the body, shaped to direct a smooth, controlled stream of liquid into a cup. The spout is usually angled slightly downward and may have a short “lip” to prevent drips. |
| **Handle** | An ergonomically placed grip—often on the opposite side of the spout—allowing the teapot to be lifted and poured without burning the hand. Handles can be a simple loop, an arched bar, or an elaborate decorative element. |
| **Infuser (optional)** | Some teapots include an internal basket or mesh strainer that holds loose tea leaves, keeping them separate from the brewed liquid. In other designs, the tea leaves are placed directly in the water, and the teapot is strained when pouring. |
| **Material & Insulation** | - **Porcelain/ceramic**: retains heat well, easy to clean, great for delicate teas. <br> - **Glass**: lets you watch the steeping process; often used for visual appeal. <br> - **Cast iron**: excellent heat retention, sometimes with a lacquered interior to prevent rust. <br> - **Stainless steel**: durable, modern look, often double‑walled for insulation. |
| **Aesthetic styles** | - **Japanese (kyūsu)**: typically small, with a side handle and a short, narrow spout. <br> - **English (silver‑plated or bone china)**: larger, often with an elegant, rounded silhouette and a longer spout. <br> - **Chinese yun (yún)**: a vase‑shaped body with a wide, short spout and a decorative “cloud” motif. <br> - **Modern minimalist**: clean lines, neutral colors, sometimes made of matte metal or clear glass. |

### Functional Characteristics

1. **Heat Retention**: The body’s thickness and material affect how long the tea stays hot. Double‑walled glass or cast‑iron teapots keep the brew warm for longer periods.
2. **Steeping Control**: A well‑designed lid prevents excess steam loss, while the interior shape can promote even water circulation around the tea leaves for consistent extraction.
3. **Pour Precision**: The spout’s geometry determines how accurately you can pour, especially important for delicate teas where you want a steady, non‑dripping flow.
4. **Cleaning Ease**: Smooth, glazed interiors are easier to rinse; porous clay or unglazed stoneware may absorb flavors and require more careful cleaning.

### Cultural & Symbolic Aspects

- **Ritual**: In many traditions, the act of preparing tea with a teapot is a ritual that emphasizes mindfulness, hospitality, and the sharing of warmth.
- **Artistry**: Hand‑painted porcelain teapots can be cherished heirlooms, and some artists create limited‑edition pieces that blend utility with fine art.
- **Collectibility**: Antique teapots from the 18th‑ and 19th‑centuries, especially those with intricate silverwork or unique shapes, are prized by collectors.

### Quick Visual Description

Imagine a gently rounded white porcelain body, about the size of a small melon. A glossy, lid with a tiny knob sits snugly on top. On the right side, a curved, ergonomic handle arches upward, inviting your hand. Opposite the handle, a sleek, slightly flared spout extends outward, its lip polished to a smooth finish that lets a thin ribbon of amber tea flow cleanly into a waiting cup. The whole piece exudes a blend of simplicity and elegance, ready to transform hot water into a comforting brew.
