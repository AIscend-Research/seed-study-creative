The sky is a deepening bruise of indigo, streaked with the last thin ribbons of amber and rose that the sun has reluctantly pulled from the horizon. A thin veil of mist curls out of the sea, catching the fading light and turning it into a pearlescent haze that clings to the jagged shoreline.

Rocks—massive, weather‑worn monoliths—jut out of the water in irregular clusters, their surfaces slick with salt spray. The tide rolls over them in a steady, rhythmic pulse, spilling foam into crevices that glitter like scattered diamonds before being swallowed again. Shadows tumble across the basalt faces, highlighting the striations and moss‑dotted fissures that have been carved by centuries of wind and wave.

The ocean itself is a restless, darkening slate, its surface mottled with the occasional glint of silver where a lone fish jumps or a distant gull brushes the water. A low, steady wind hisses over the cliffs, tugging at the frayed edges of sea‑weed and sending the occasional spray to kiss the air with a briny tang.

In the distance, the silhouette of a weathered lighthouse stands on a higher outcrop, its lantern beginning to pulse a soft, amber glow. The light flickers in time with the wave’s breath, casting a thin, golden circle that dances on the water’s edge before fading into the gloom.

Below, a narrow, pebble‑strewn beach curls around the base of the cliffs, its dark stones glistening with residual moisture. Small crabs scuttle hurriedly headlong into the tide pools that form in the rock basins, their shells catching the dimming light like tiny scarabs.

As the last sliver of sun slips below the horizon, the world seems to hold its breath. The colors shift from warm golds to cool blues, and the rocky coast, bathed in a hush of twilight, exudes a quiet, timeless majesty—an ancient stage where sea and stone meet under the ever‑watchful eye of the gathering night.
