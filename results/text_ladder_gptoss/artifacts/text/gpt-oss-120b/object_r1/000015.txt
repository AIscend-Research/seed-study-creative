A teapot is a small, vessel designed especially for steeping tea leaves and then pouring the brewed liquid into cups. Though its exact shape can vary widely, a classic teapot typically includes the following elements:

| Part | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Body (or kettle)** | The main, rounded container that holds the water and tea leaves. It’s often bulbous or slightly oval, allowing enough volume for a few cups of tea while keeping the liquid warm. |
| **Spout** | A tapered, often slightly curved nozzle that directs the flow of liquid. It usually has a smooth, lip‑finished edge to prevent drips and a small internal conduit that guides the tea out cleanly. |
| **Handle** | A sturdy, ergonomic loop positioned opposite the spout. It’s large enough for a comfortable grip, keeping your hand safely away from the hot metal or ceramic. |
| **Lid** | A fitted cover that sits atop the body. It may be hinged or removable, often featuring a small knob or a decorative finial for easy lifting. The lid seals the pot, retaining heat while allowing the steam to escape through a tiny vent. |
| **Infuser (optional)** | Some teapots contain a removable mesh basket or built‑in strainer that holds loose tea leaves, letting the water circulate freely while keeping the leaves from spilling into the cup. |

### Common Materials

| Material | Characteristics |
|----------|-----------------|
| **Ceramic (porcelain, stoneware)** | Excellent heat retention, can be glazed in countless colors and patterns; heavy and brittle. |
| **Metal (stainless steel, cast iron, aluminum)** | Very durable, conducts heat quickly; cast‑iron versions often have an enamel coating to prevent rust. |
| **Glass** | Transparent, allowing you to watch the tea steep; usually heat‑resistant borosilicate. |
| **Clay (yixing, earthenware)** | Porous and can absorb flavors over time, prized for certain tea traditions. |

### Visual Overview

Imagine a gently curving, white porcelain body that bulges slightly in the middle, reminiscent of a small, graceful egg. A sleek silver spout extends from the side, its tip narrowing to a perfectly rounded lip. Opposite the spout, a broad, knurled handle arches upward, offering a comfortable place for the fingers. Resting atop the kettle is a round lid with a tiny knob shaped like a tea leaf, completing the silhouette.

### Functional Highlights

- **Steeping**: Hot water is poured into the body, where it meets the tea leaves (either loose or in a tea bag). The leaves release their flavors over a few minutes.
- **Heat Retention**: The material and shape help keep the brew hot long after it’s poured.
- **Pouring**: The spout’s design ensures a smooth, steady stream, making it easy to fill cups without spilling.
- **Aesthetic Appeal**: Teapots often serve as decorative pieces on kitchen shelves or dining tables, reflecting cultural motifs, seasonal colors, or artisanal craftsmanship.

In short, a teapot is both a practical tool for brewing tea and a timeless object of design, blending function with visual charm. Whether made of porcelain, cast iron, or glass, its essential purpose remains the same: to turn hot water and tea leaves into a comforting, aromatic cup of tea.
