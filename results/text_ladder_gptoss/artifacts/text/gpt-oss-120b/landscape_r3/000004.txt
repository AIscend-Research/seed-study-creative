The sun has slipped just past the horizon, and the sky swells with the bruised purples and smoky blues of twilight. A ragged strip of coast stretches out like a weather‑worn spine, jagged cliffs of dark basalt rising from the sea in uneven, staggered steps. Their surfaces are scarred with ancient fissures and pitted with lichen‑spotted patches that catch the last amber glow, turning the rock faces into a mottled tapestry of gold and slate.

Below the cliffs, the tide rolls in and out over a frothy carpet of white‑capped waves. The water, now a deepening indigo, reflects the fading light in shimmering ribbons, while the occasional spray catches the dying sun and bursts into brief, fiery sparks. Small tidal pools cling to the rock ledges, their glassy surfaces mirroring the sky like tiny, trembling mirrors.

A thin veil of mist begins to rise from the surf, curling over the lower cliffs and veiling the occasional outcrop in a soft, ghostly hush. The wind sighs through the crevices, carrying a salty tang and the distant call of a lone gull circling high above, its wings silhouetted against the dimming heavens.

Further inland, scrubby vegetation clings to the rocky soil—stubborn grasses, twisted sea‑spray bushes, and a few hardy wildflowers that stubbornly push through cracks, their petals catching a final flash of amber before the night fully settles. The air grows cooler, and the landscape seems to hold its breath, balanced between the day's last light and the encroaching dark.

In this moment, the rocky coast at dusk feels both raw and serene—a rugged frontier illuminated by fleeting colors, where stone, sea, and sky converge in a quiet, timeless tableau.
