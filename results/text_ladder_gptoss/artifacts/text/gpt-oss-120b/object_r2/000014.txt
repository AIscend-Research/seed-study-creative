A **ceramic teapot** is a charming, functional piece of kitchenware that blends practicality with aesthetic appeal. Here’s a detailed look at its typical features:

---

## 1. Overall Shape  
- **Body (or kettle):** Usually rounded or slightly oval, tapering gently toward the base for stability.  
- **Spout:** A slender, curved outlet that directs the flow of tea. It may feature a short, flared lip to prevent drips.  
- **Handle:** An ergonomic, hollow handle (often shaped like a swooping arc) that stays cool to the touch, allowing easy pouring.  
- **Lid:** A shallow dome or gently sloping cover, often with a small knob or a decorative pull for easy removal.  

## 2. Materials & Construction  
- **Clay Base:** Made from ceramic clay (stoneware, porcelain, or earthenware) that’s fired at high temperatures, giving it strength and heat retention.  
- **Glaze:** A vitrified coating that makes the surface waterproof, easy to clean, and often adds color or pattern. Glazes can be glossy, matte, or somewhere in between.  
- **Porosity:** High-quality ceramic teapots are low‑porosity, meaning they won’t absorb flavors or odors over time.  

## 3. Visual Details  
- **Color Palette:** Ranges from classic whites and creams to deep blues, greens, reds, and pastel tones. Some teapots showcase intricate hand‑painted motifs—floral, geometric, or abstract designs.  
- **Texture:** The exterior can be smooth and glossy, or feature a textured finish like speckles, crackle glaze, or a hand‑applied slip that gives a tactile feel.  
- **Patterns:** Common decorative elements include:  
  - **Traditional motifs:** Chinese dragons, Japanese cherry blossoms, or English floral patterns.  
  - **Modern designs:** Minimalist lines, abstract splashes, or monochrome geometry.  
- **Accents:** Metallic rims, gilt lettering, or small ceramic charms (e.g., a tiny tea leaf) can add a touch of elegance.

## 4. Functional Features  
- **Heat Retention:** Ceramic’s natural ability to hold heat keeps tea warm for longer than metal or glass.  
- **Infusion Space:** Many have a built‑in or removable infuser basket at the bottom, allowing loose‑leaf tea to steep while keeping leaves separate from the poured liquid.  
- **Pour Control:** The spout’s shape, combined with a well‑designed lip, gives a smooth, steady pour, reducing splatter.  
- **Ease of Cleaning:** The glazed interior prevents tea stains; most are dishwasher‑safe, though hand‑washing extends longevity for delicate glazes.

## 5. Size & Capacity  
- **Small (single‑serve):** 150–200 ml – perfect for personal use or tasting sessions.  
- **Medium (standard):** 300–500 ml – ideal for 2–4 cups of tea.  
- **Large (family or entertaining):** 600–900 ml or more – suited for gatherings.

## 6. Cultural Touches  
- **Chinese Yixing Teapots:** Made from a special porous clay that absorbs tea flavors, prized for gongfu brewing.  
- **Japanese Kyusetsu:** Often with a simple, rustic glaze, designed for Japanese green teas.  
- **British Afternoon Teapots:** Classic English patterns (e.g., floral “Blue Willow”) for serving black tea with milk.

### Example Description

*Imagine a medium‑sized, ivory‑glazed ceramic teapot standing about 12 cm tall. Its rounded body is finished with a subtle, crackle glaze that reveals a faint honey‑colored network beneath the smooth surface. A graceful, slightly curved spout arches toward a polished, silver‑tone lip that catches the light. The handle is a gentle, hollow sweep resembling a leaf, staying cool even when the tea inside is steaming hot. A modest, dome‑shaped lid crowns the pot, centered with a tiny, hand‑painted cherry blossom in soft pink. Inside, the glossy glaze is pristine, ensuring each pour delivers clear, fragrant tea without a hint of residue.*

---

**In summary**, a ceramic teapot is more than just a vessel; it’s a blend of artistry, tradition, and practical design that enhances the ritual of tea brewing and serving. Whether you prefer understated elegance or vibrant ornamentation, there’s a ceramic teapot to suit every taste.
