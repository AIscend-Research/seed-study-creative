A teapot is a small, usually rounded vessel designed for steeping tea leaves in hot water and then pouring the brewed tea into a cup. Its key components include:

- **Body (or kettle)**: The main, bulbous part that holds the water and tea leaves. It’s often made of porcelain, ceramic, glass, metal (such as stainless steel or cast iron), or even clay. The interior is smooth to allow easy cleaning, while the exterior may be plain or decorated with patterns, glazes, or engravings.

- **Spout**: A tapered outlet extending from the body, shaped to direct the liquid in a smooth, controlled stream. A well‑designed spout minimizes drips and helps prevent the tea from splashing when poured.

- **Handle**: Usually positioned opposite the spout, the handle allows the user to lift and tilt the teapot safely. It is often ergonomically curved and may be made from the same material as the body or from a heat‑insulating material like wood, silicone, or a metal that stays cool to the touch.

- **Lid**: A snug-fitting cover that sits on top of the body. The lid keeps the heat inside while the tea steeps and often has a small knob or handle for easy removal. Some lids have a small vent or a tiny opening to release steam.

- **Infuser (optional)**: Many modern teapots include a removable mesh or perforated basket that holds loose‑leaf tea. This keeps the leaves from scattering into the brewed tea when it’s poured. Some teapots have built‑in stainless‑steel infusers that sit inside the body.

**Aesthetic and functional variations**

- **Traditional designs**: Classic English bone‑china teapots often feature floral motifs, gilt edges, and a delicate, translucent white finish. Japanese “kyūsu” teapots are generally smaller, with a side handle and a thin spout for precise pouring. Chinese Yixing clay teapots are hand‑crafted, porous, and prized for enhancing the flavor of certain teas over time.

- **Modern designs**: Contemporary teapots may showcase sleek, minimalist lines, bold colors, or innovative materials like double‑walled glass that lets you see the tea brew while keeping the exterior cool.

- **Size**: Teapots range from single‑serve “personal” sizes (holding about 150 ml/5 oz) to large family‑style models that can accommodate 1 L (about 34 oz) or more.

- **Usage considerations**: A good teapot retains heat to keep the tea warm but also allows you to control steeping time—many teapots are designed so you can easily remove the infuser or pour out the tea while leaving the leaves behind.

In essence, a teapot is both a functional kitchen tool for brewing tea and a decorative object that often reflects cultural traditions and personal style.
